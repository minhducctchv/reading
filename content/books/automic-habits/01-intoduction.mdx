# Introduction

## Summary
The author, James Clear, shares his personal story of recovering from a terrible baseball injury in high school. Through small, consistent habits, he rebuilt his life, succeeded in college sports, and achieved top academic honors. Later, he used these same habit-building strategies to grow a successful business and write this book. The book offers a practical, step-by-step guide to building good habits and breaking bad ones, based on the four laws of behavior change.

## My Story
On the final day of my sophomore year of high school, a baseball bat hit me in the face. It broke my nose, fractured my skull, and shattered my eye sockets. My brain swelled rapidly, and I lost consciousness. 

A helicopter flew me to a larger hospital. There, doctors put me into a medically induced coma to save my life. My parents were terrified. They had been to the same hospital ten years earlier when my sister had cancer. 

The next morning, my breathing improved. The doctors brought me out of the coma. I had to wait a week for surgery. My recovery was very slow and painful. I had double vision for weeks. I could not drive a car for eight months. 

A year later, I tried to return to baseball. But I was cut from the varsity team. It was a humiliating and sad experience. Even so, I still wanted to be a great player. I knew I had to make it happen myself.

## How I Learned About Habits
Going to Denison University changed my life. I made the baseball team, even though I was at the bottom of the roster. 

I decided to get my life in order. I started going to bed early. I kept my room clean. I lifted weights several times a week. These small improvements gave me a sense of control. They brought my confidence back.

A habit is a routine or behavior that you do regularly. By sticking to these small routines, I saw amazing results. By my junior year, I became the team captain. In my senior year, I won top athletic and academic honors at the university. 

I did not become a professional baseball player. But I fulfilled my potential. I learned a critical lesson: small changes lead to remarkable results if you stick with them for years. Your habits determine your success. 

## How and Why I Wrote This Book
In 2012, I started publishing articles on my website, jamesclear.com. I wrote about my personal experiments with habits. I posted two times a week. 

This simple writing habit changed my career. My email list grew quickly. By 2015, I had 200,000 subscribers and signed a book deal to write *Atomic Habits*. 

As my audience grew, my business grew too. People asked me to speak at major companies. My articles appeared in famous magazines like *Time* and *Forbes*. I also launched the Habits Academy to train leaders and organizations. Thousands of people joined. 

## How This Book Will Benefit You
I had to use small habits to recover from my injury, succeed in sports, and build a business. Small habits helped me reach my potential. I believe this book will help you reach your potential, too.

In this book, you will find a step-by-step plan for building better habits for a lifetime. It is an operating manual, not an academic paper. It combines the best ideas from biology, neuroscience, philosophy, and psychology. 

The core of this book is my four-step model of habits. These steps are cue, craving, response, and reward. From these steps, I created the Four Laws of Behavior Change. 

Behavior changes from moment to moment. But this book focuses on what stays the same. The principles in this book will work no matter what your goal is. If you want to improve your health, money, productivity, or relationships, this book is your guide.
